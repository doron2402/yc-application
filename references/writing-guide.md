# How to Write a YC Application

See `accepted-example.md` for a real accepted application, annotated.

## How partners read

A partner reads a few hundred applications per batch, often a couple of minutes each. They skim the 50-character description and "What is your company going to make?" first, then the founders, then progress. If they can't tell what you make in ten seconds, the rest barely gets read. Write for someone smart, busy, and outside your industry.

They are deciding three things:
1. **Are these founders formidable?** Do they build fast, figure things out, and get things done that others couldn't?
2. **Is the product clear, and does someone want it?**
3. **Is there evidence?** Users, revenue, growth rate, or an insight that's unusual and true.

## Style rules

- **Lead with the answer.** First sentence answers the question. Context after, if needed.
- **Plain words.** No "revolutionize", "disrupt", "leverage", "synergy", "seamless", "next-generation", "AI-powered platform", "one-stop shop". Say what the thing does.
- **Specific over general.** "Dental clinics in Texas" beats "healthcare providers". "Sarah, a nurse at Kaiser, used it 40 times in her first week" beats "users love it".
- **Numbers, with time.** "1,200 weekly active users, up from 300 in August" beats "1,200 users". Growth rate matters more than size at this stage.
- **Short.** Most strong answers are 1–4 sentences. Long answers signal unclear thinking.
- **Honest.** "No users yet. We've done 30 interviews and 8 dentists asked when they can start" is fine. Inflated numbers are an automatic no, and partners check.
- **Don't hedge.** Cut "we believe", "we hope to", "potentially". State it.
- **No buzzword stacking.** One clear noun beats three adjectives.

## Field-by-field

**50-character description.** What it does, not why it matters. Form: "[Product] for [who]" or "[Verb] [thing] for [who]". Count the characters; ≤ 50 including spaces.
- Good: "Scheduling software for dental clinics" (38)
- Good: "Stripe for cross-border payroll in LatAm" (40)
- Bad: "Revolutionizing healthcare with AI" (says nothing)

**What is your company going to make?** First sentence = what it is and who it's for. Second = what the user does with it / how it works. Third (optional) = the one proof point or why it's better. A smart 12-year-old should get it.

**Founders: most impressive thing.** One concrete achievement per founder, with a number or an outcome. "Built a Minecraft server that got 20K players at 15" is better than "passionate about building".

**Hacked a non-computer system** (founder profile, video, or interview; no longer a form box, but a great story to have ready). A short true story: the system, the rule or assumption, what you did, what you got. Partners read it for resourcefulness, not for rule-breaking for its own sake. Keep it legal and ethical.

**How long have you known each other.** Years, how you met, and what you've built or shipped together. Co-founders who just met are a risk; say what you've done together to de-risk it.

**Who writes code.** Name the founder(s). If a non-founder wrote any of it, say so honestly and say which founder owns the code now. Partners care a lot about founders building the product themselves.

**How far along / Progress.** Dates, users, revenue, growth rate. "We launched March 3. 412 users, 140 weekly active, growing 15% a week."

**Active users / paying / top payer.** Define "active". Give total, paying, and the biggest customer by name (or type) and what they pay. A single big payer is strong evidence; say it.

**Revenue.** Monthly, not cumulative, not GMV (YC says this explicitly). Last few months, month by month. Break down sources if any of it is consulting or another product: hiding that is a red flag. Separate recurring from one-time, and paid pilots from LOIs.

**Tech stack.** Concrete: languages, frameworks, infra, AI models, and AI coding tools. If there's a coding-agent session the founder is proud of, suggest attaching it: it shows how fast the team builds.

**Bank / burn / runway.** Exact numbers. Short runway isn't disqualifying; vagueness is.

**Why this idea / domain expertise.** The personal origin: what you saw, where, and why you're unusually suited to build it. Founder-market fit in 2–3 sentences.

**How do you know people need it.** The single strongest piece of evidence first: behavior beats opinion. Users who pay, come back daily, built workarounds, or begged for access.

**Competitors + what you understand that they don't.** Name real competitors and the status quo (spreadsheets, agencies, doing nothing). Then the insight: something non-obvious and true about customers, distribution, technology, or timing. "We're better" is not an insight.

**How do or will you make money.** Who pays, the price, and bottom-up math to a big number: "Clinics pay $300/month. There are 200K dental clinics in the US, so $720M/year in the US alone; dental is our wedge into all outpatient clinics."

**Why YC / how did you hear.** Short and true. Mention an alum by name if one encouraged you.

**Location.** Where you live now, where you'll be after YC, one sentence on why.

## Founder video script (1 minute, required)

YC calls the video "an important part of the application". It is the partner's first look at the team.


- All founders on camera, sitting together, phone camera is fine.
- 0:00–0:15: each founder says name and what they do on the team (one line each).
- 0:15–0:45: one founder says what the company makes, in the same words as the application, and the best proof point (users, revenue, growth).
- 0:45–1:00: one sentence on why this team.
- No slides, no music, no script reading from a screen, no pitch-deck voice. Talk like you'd talk to a friend.
- Write it as spoken lines, ~140 words total.

## Common mistakes

- The product description is about the vision, not the product.
- Traction stated without dates or growth.
- Every answer ends with a sales line ("…and we're excited to change the world").
- Hiding that a founder is part-time or that code is outsourced.
- Huge top-down TAM ("1% of a $500B market").
- Answers copy-pasted from the deck, written for investors, not for a partner skimming.
