# An Accepted Application: Per Diem (YC W21)

Per Diem was accepted into YC Winter 2021, applying before launch, with two founders working nights and weekends. This file shows what carried that application and what it would do even better on today's form. Use it to calibrate: an application doesn't need to be polished to get in; it needs a real team, a real problem with a real customer, and honest numbers.

## What made it work

**1. A team with real shared history.** "[We] have been friends since we met working at OpenTable in 2015. We worked on Guest Center, OpenTable's reservation management platform. I was a product manager and Doron led one of our engineering teams." Years together, a shipped product, and a clear PM + engineering split. They also listed three earlier concepts they'd built together, which showed a long-running partnership, not a team formed for the application.
→ *Ask every team: how long, how did you meet, and what have you built together?*

**2. Founder-market fit stated with credentials, not adjectives.** Both founders came from OpenTable, one led consumer product at Resy through its acquisition by Amex, and they named their go-to-market wedge (food & beverage) as the industry they knew.
→ *Push for the concrete line on the résumé that makes this team the obvious one.*

**3. A specific origin customer with numbers.** "My sister and her husband are the owners of Norman's Farm Market … online CSA business, which had 1000 customers already and $1m in ARR." One named business, a real pain, real numbers. Then the generalization: lawn care, grocers, barber shops, cleaners.
→ *Always get one named customer and their numbers before generalizing.*

**4. A vivid proof of the status quo's pain.** "There's a 3 hour YouTube video on how to add subscriptions to your Shopify account!" One concrete detail beats a paragraph about complexity.
→ *Ask: what's the most absurd thing people do today to work around this?*

**5. Honest progress, even when early.** "Are people using your product? No." Part-time ("nights and weekends") stated openly. Alongside that: $5K/month revenue for two months, LOIs from 5 merchants doing over $5M in online revenue, a dated beta launch, and the product built for under $3K.
→ *Honesty plus whatever real evidence exists. Never inflate.*

**6. Money math anchored on a real customer.** "1% of transactional revenue … Our first merchant generates $1m … which will equate to $10k in revenue from that customer." Then the bigger vision: a consumer site and app that aggregates demand and builds local network effects, at a higher take rate.
→ *Price × a real customer first, then the path to a much bigger business.*

**7. A competitor answer with an insider insight.** On the biggest threat (Google): "from my experience at OpenTable/Resy, Google prefers intermediaries to power the underlying service." That's an insight earned from experience, not a feature comparison.

**8. A memorable human detail.** The "surprising thing" answer: once they stopped fundraising conversations to focus on launch, VCs got more persistent. Short, true, a bit funny.

## What would be stronger on today's form

The skill should push for these, using this application as the "before":

- **50-character description:** "Per Diem is a subscription ecommerce platform." spends 12 characters on the name and is generic. Better: "Subscriptions for local shops, no code needed" (45). Say who it's for.
- **"What do you understand that they don't?"** The answer was a market claim (underserved businesses under $1M). A sharper insight is a non-obvious *why*, e.g. that local businesses want pickup-first subscriptions that ecommerce tools don't model.
- **Evidence:** "they're desperate to find new sources of revenue" is an opinion. The LOIs and $5K MRR were the real evidence and belonged in "How do you know people need it".
- **Market size:** it gave a 300-merchant first-year plan, not a bottom-up total. Add: how many such merchants exist × revenue per merchant.
- **Who writes code:** the code was written by Upwork contractors with a founder reviewing it. The current form asks this directly, and partners today care a lot about founders building the product themselves. Answer honestly, and show the founders own the core.
- **Why YC:** "launch Per Diem the right way" is generic. Name the person, event, or specific reason.
- **Founder video:** now required and called "an important part of the application".
